# Campaign notes

Read from the code, not from a playthrough. The number on the card is the level index plus one. All 30 cards are meant to be clearable. An extra miss can drop the third star and still leave the clear.

Loadout is **bombs / shells / mines / VOG**. The gun is unlimited and does not count as a release. Spotter, kamikaze, and mine-haul cards carry no stores: the tool is the mark, the drone, or a mine from the cache.

## Height and the drop

Scatter is applied when the round leaves the nose. The gun does not scatter.

| Altitude | Bomb and mine | Shell | VOG |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| up to 10 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 12 | ±2 | ±3 | 0 |
| 16 | ±3 | ±4 | ±2 |
| 20 | ±4 | ±5 | ±2 |
| 30 | ±6 | ±7 | ±3 |
| 40 and above | up to ±10 | up to ±10 | up to ±5 |

Bomb and mine use altitude / 5, a shell uses altitude / 4, VOG uses altitude / 8. The cap is 10 pixels. Below two pixels the drop stays on the nose.

Radii to compare: tank hatch 6, mine 10, VOG 12, bomb 14, shell 22, tank hull 28. A low pass still finds the hatch. From 30 a bomb on the hatch is a toss, and a bomb on a hull or a truck is not. VOG stays true until about 16.

A pine is solid only below 6. Cruise and every mission start sit above that, so a high pass clears the wood. Drop into the crown and the airframe is lost, the same way a ram ends a sortie.

## Battery

The pack is not a stopwatch, and releasing a round does not spend it. Forward flight drains it. Hover drains about two thirds of that rate. The rate comes from the card's own clock: a sortie flown the whole way, with no hover, still has about thirty-five points left when the clock runs out, which is the battery star. Hovering to aim leaves more. A dead radio still takes a little extra. The mine quad, sitting on a charge pad, fills the pack.

## Trucks

A bomb from the usual pass falls for about a second. At road speed the truck moves just past the 14-pixel blast in that second, so a round aimed at the truck as it is now lands behind it. A spotter salvo waits four seconds, so those trucks drive slower: the mark still has to lead, and the truck stays in the glass. Convoy, the snow road, and the escort start farther from the line they are driving to, so the faster run does not end the card before you arrive. The push keeps the infantry's walk, because one mark is meant to cover the truck and the two men beside it. Lane, dart, and the low spotter trucks pace six tiles either side of where they started.

## Instruments

Labels stay up through interference. Once the radio loss reaches 8, and whenever the glass is the full noise picture, the figures become dashes. Score, altitude, stores, hull, battery, signal, and the remaining marks all read as unknown. The labels and the static icon stay.

## Winter cards

26 and 27 were already their own sorties. Their seating was left as it was.

28 is no longer one truck. The truck still drives north and the card fails if it arrives. Three men stand on the mud beside that road. Two of them share a grenade. A bomb on the truck does not reach them.

29 is no longer three tanks. The tanks hold still on three islands to the north, and they are the nearer job. Four men stand together on their own mud well south of the pad, inside one grenade. A bomb or a mine on a hull does not reach them, and the hatch is not inside their rifles.

30 is no longer one officer. The officer is on the mud, two men beside him inside one grenade, two a step further out on the same post. The clear is all five. The post is watched: below 20 inside a circle of radius 112 around the post, the guns fire. Above 20 the aircraft is gone from them.

## How even it is

The campaign is no longer "training with forty bombs" and then "winter with a handful". Each card is stocked for its own task.

Easy and even: 1, 2, 3, 7. One plain target, or more blasts than targets.

The middle, and most of the list: 4–6, 8, 11–14, 16, 17, 20, 21, 23, 25–28, 30. A miss is forgiven. The third star on 4 and 25 is tight: one extra bomb drops it, the clear remains. 30 adds the height watch.

Harder, and still clearable: 9, 10, 15, 18, 19, 22, 24, 29. Either many targets in a row, or a tank that wants you low, or guns that punish the low pass.

Gaps this reading can see without a flight:

- Cards 10, 15, and 29 cannot be closed with one kind of charge from high. Four mines are eight hull points, and three tanks need nine. Six bombs are also short of three hulls. Hatch, or a mix. A player who stays high and never uses the hatch will feel the card is impossible, even though the stores are enough.
- 4 and 25 are easy to clear and easy to lose the star on. That is the narrowest clear in the campaign.
- 27 is two sites. A grenade on one man of a pair reaches the other. The rifles do not see a pass above 20.
- 30 is stealth. The gun still takes the five, and the post is watched. Below 20 in the circle the guns fire. Above 20 the pass is quiet.
- Spotter cards 8, 9, 14, 18, and 20 are not balanced by stores. The work is holding the mark. The spotter cannot climb, so a pine on the track is a wall until you go around it.
- Winter glass cannot be switched to night or heat.

Spread is about 1 to 4. There is no 5. The middle outweighs the ends. The widest stores are the headquarters, the trench, and the bunkers. The thinnest are the single truck and the armour if the hatch is ignored.
